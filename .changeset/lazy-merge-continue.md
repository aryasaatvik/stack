`stack merge --auto --through` now repairs lazily and can resume with `--continue`. Previously every landed root triggered a full-chain rebase+force-push of every remaining descendant, so a 17-root campaign fanned out to ~135 rebase+push operations (each force-push re-triggering a code-review bot) and a single replay conflict ended the campaign with no path back but re-running the whole command. Repair is now lazy: after a root lands, only the next root on the `--through` chain is rebased+pushed (grandchildren and off-chain siblings wait their turn — sound under squash-merge, where trunk's tree after landing P equals P's tip), with one final full repair pass over whatever stays open when the through-target lands. This makes a linear chain cost one rebase+push per branch instead of O(n²). A new `--eager` flag restores the old full-chain repair after every landing. Campaign progress is journaled in `.git/stack/campaign.json` at each landing boundary, so `stack merge --continue` resumes from the recorded next root after a manual conflict fix — trusting the already-merged roots instead of re-repairing everything. `--continue` errors with no saved campaign and rejects a branch argument or `--through` (the campaign owns those). The campaign journal is independent of the undo journal: `undo` still restores the last landing while the campaign survives for `--continue`.
